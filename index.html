Atmospheric Processes and Environmental Effects of Highly Reactive Gases

We study the sources, chemical transformation, and removal of highly reactive gases, and how these processes affect atmospheric oxidizing capacity and the formation of ozone and secondary particulate matter.

We examine how reaction pathways shift under different emission and meteorological conditions and how those shifts affect regional air quality.

Ocean–Atmosphere Chemical Interactions

We study the exchange of trace gases and particles between the ocean and atmosphere, how marine emissions shape the chemistry of the marine boundary layer, and how atmospheric deposition alters surface ocean chemistry.

By comparing air–sea exchange over the open ocean and near the coast, we examine how pollution transported from land interacts with marine emissions to shape coastal atmospheric composition.

Drivers and Mitigation of Ozone Pollution

We examine how ground-level ozone pollution develops by distinguishing the roles of precursor emissions, photochemistry, meteorology, and regional transport.

We investigate how ozone responds to reductions in nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds across regions and seasons to inform targeted control strategies.
