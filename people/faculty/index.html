Faculty

Yuqiang Zhang, Ph.D.

Professor

Email yuqiang.zhang@sdu.edu.cn

Shandong University faculty profile

Yuqiang Zhang earned his Ph.D. at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. He uses atmospheric modeling and data science to study how climate change and air pollution affect human health, with particular interests in pollutant transport, atmospheric deposition, and urban air quality.

Qinyi Li, Ph.D.

Professor

Email qinyi.li@sdu.edu.cn

Shandong University faculty profile

Qinyi Li earned his Ph.D. at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. He develops regional and global models to study reactive halogen and nitrogen chemistry and understand how these processes affect air quality and climate.

Liubin Huang, Ph.D.

Research Professor

Email hliubin@sdu.edu.cn

Shandong University faculty profile

Liubin Huang completed postdoctoral research at the University of California San Diego. He studies multiphase aerosol chemistry, how secondary aerosols form and change, and the effects of marine aerosols on the atmosphere and climate.

Zheng Zong, Ph.D.

Research Professor

Email zzong@sdu.edu.cn

Shandong University faculty profile

Zheng Zong earned his doctorate in environmental science at the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences. He uses isotope measurements to trace the sources of air pollution, with a focus on reactive nitrogen species such as nitrate, ammonium, and nitrous acid.

Hengqing Shen, Ph.D.

Research Professor

Email hqshen@sdu.edu.cn

Shandong University faculty profile

Hengqing Shen combines field observations, laboratory experiments, and modeling to study marine emissions and multiphase reactions involving reactive nitrogen, halogens, and carbonyl compounds. His work examines how this chemistry affects atmospheric oxidation and secondary pollution in coastal regions.

Yujiao Zhu, Ph.D.

Associate Research Professor

Email zhuyujiao@sdu.edu.cn

Shandong University faculty profile

Yujiao Zhu earned her doctorate in environmental science at Ocean University of China. She studies how atmospheric particles form and grow and how these processes influence clouds and climate.
